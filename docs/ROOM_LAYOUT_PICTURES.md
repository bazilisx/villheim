# Room layouts seen in pictures

What actual game interiors show about where the big furniture stands, which way it faces, where people walk and
where the clutter goes. Written to check the rules in `docs/ROOM_LAYOUT.md` and
`tools/blueprint-lab/data/room-layouts.json` against real designs.

## How this was done

About 56 browser screenshots, many of them grids of 2 to 6 pictures, so 79 pictures in all. Sources were in-game
screenshots on guide sites and wikis (UESP, Gamer Guides, Game Rant, Prima, VG247), official environment art on
ArtStation (Tim Coddens and Jef Simons for BG3, Len de Gracia and Andrzej Dybowski for The Witcher 3), Witcher 3
concept art reposted on Pinterest, Valheim player builds on valheimians.com and Pinterest, ESO and WoW housing
articles and Enshrouded build posts. Reddit was not used.

Limits:

- Most shots are three-quarter views. Distances are judged by eye against people, chairs and tables. Treat every
  number here as rough.
- Concept art (several Witcher 3 pictures) is marked. It shows the intended layout, which may differ from the built room.
- Valheim, ESO and Enshrouded pictures are player builds. They show what players do with the same kind of pieces
  Villheim uses. They are player layouts, and the studio rooms carry more weight. The WoW pictures are Blizzard preview homes.
- Counts below are counts of pictures or pieces I could judge. A room type seen 4 times gives a hint only.

## Patterns by room type

Each pattern gives how often I saw it and what it means for `data/room-layouts.json`.

### Sleeping rooms and guest rooms

I could judge 22 beds or sleeping platforms (BG3 4, Witcher 3 5 including two platform rows, Skyrim 10, Valheim 2,
WoW 1).

- **Every bed touches a wall: 22 of 22.** None stands free in the middle. Confirms "walls are for furniture".
- **Three distinct bed placements, tied to bed size and room:**
  - Double bed, head centered on the end or far wall, both long sides open, a nightstand on each side: 6 of 6 double
    beds (Skyrim 4, Valheim 1, WoW 1 likely). This is the "far-wall" scheme with `sideAccess`. Confirms it, and adds
    "two nightstands, one each side" for double beds.
  - Single bed in a corner with its long side to a wall, an eave or a screen: 8 of 12 single beds or platforms that
    stood alone in a room (BG3 Fraygo, W3 Velen nook, W3 peasant house, Skellige hut platform, Skellige longhouse
    platforms, Skyrim Breezehome small room, Candlehearth guest room, Valheim cozy bedroom). **Contradicts**
    `orient.bed.sideToWall: [0.1, 0.3]` for single beds and small rooms. Suggest drawing `sideToWall` from bed size:
    about 0.6 to 0.8 for a single bed in a room under about 4 m wide or under an eave, 0.1 to 0.3 for a double bed.
  - Several beds side by side on one wall, heads to that wall, 1 to 2 m apart with a nightstand, cabinet or desk
    between: 2 of 2 multi-bed rooms (BG3 Last Light Inn infirmary, Skyrim Hearthfire children's room). The
    `guest.rows` scheme puts beds on facing long walls; the pictures put them on the same wall. Suggest adding a
    "one-wall row" scheme and a bed gap of 1.0 to 2.0 m with a small piece between, in place of the 0.7 m minimum.
- **Bed head under a window: 0 of the beds where a window was visible (about 8).** Windows sit beside the bed head or
  between beds, with a nightstand or cabinet under the window. Confirms `underWindow: -0.8`. Adds: when a window falls
  between two beds, put the nightstand under it.
- **Something at the head:** a nightstand, cabinet, stump or wardrobe next to the head in 14 of 22. **A chest at the
  foot** in 4 (Hearthfire 3, BG3 1); a chest at the side in 1. **Something hung over the head** (shields, banners, a
  shelf, fur pelts) in 10 of 22, most often Skyrim and Valheim. The current JSON has no rule for the wall above the
  bed head; this is a strong story spot.
- **Rug at the open side or the foot:** 12 of 22, round rugs at the side or a runner along the open side.
- **Bed and fire:** in sleeping rooms the fire is on another wall or absent (Last Light Inn, Bannered Mare, Hearthfire
  master). In one-room homes and longhouses the beds share the room with the fire 4 of 4 (Skellige hut, Skellige
  longhouse, Hearthfire small house, Alvor house), at about 2 to 5 m, the sleeping platforms facing it. The noble
  Witcher bedroom puts the bed right beside the fireplace, and one Hearthfire bed has its head on the back of the
  chimney. **Contradicts** a flat `nearFire: -1.2` for homes. Suggest: keep a penalty only inside the hearth ring
  (about 1.5 m), and in the `home` role let a bed along a wall face the fire from 2 to 5 m with no penalty.
- **Screens and nooks:** a bed screened by hanging rugs on a frame (W3 Velen), alcoves with furs (Skellige longhouse),
  a bed under the eave (Breezehome). 4 of 22. Confirms the `box-bed` and `eaves` schemes.
- **Floor clutter in bedrooms:** clothes or cloths on the floor by the bed in 2 BG3 rooms; a bucket, basket and stool
  by a sick bed; a sack by the bed foot in Velen. Clutter gathers at the bed side and foot and stays out of the lane.

### Hall or main room with a hearth, and longhouses

Halls, homes and longhouses with a fire that I could judge: 17 (W3 3, Skyrim 9, Valheim 3, BG3 Elfsong 2).

- **Fire in the middle of the floor: 12 of 17**; on a wall: 5 of 17 (Alvor house long wall, Hearthfire main hall end
  wall, Elfsong fireplace room, W3 dining hall, Last Light Inn infirmary). Among Norse, Skellige and Valheim rooms
  alone, the fire is in the middle in 10 of 12. Confirms `focal: fire` and a central hearth for Norse cultures. In the
  long halls it is a long fire on the long axis: 6 of 6.
- **Where the long seating goes in a long hall** (6 long halls: Jorrvaskr, Dragonsreach, Bannered Mare, Skellige
  longhouse, two Valheim longhouses):
  - Long tables or benches flank the fire on both sides, parallel to it, about 0.5 to 1.5 m from its edge, inside the
    post rows: 4 of 6 (the three Skyrim halls and the Skellige longhouse). The lane runs between the table and the
    posts, or between the table and the fire.
  - Along the long walls: 2 of 6 (both Valheim player builds).
  - What stands along the long walls in the studio halls: sleeping platforms (Skellige), raised side floors or
    platforms with small tables (Jorrvaskr, Bannered Mare), or nothing.
  - This **partly contradicts** `hall.benches` with `bench: long-walls` and `orient.bench.longWall: 2.0`. The written
    source (Hurstwic) says benches stood on the walls; the games put the eating tables by the fire and keep the walls
    for beds and side floors. Suggest a zone "fire-flank" (parallel to the fire's long side, 0.5 to 1.5 m off it) for
    tables and benches in `hall` and `tavern`, and keep `long-walls` for sleeping platforms and storage in the
    longhouse scheme. Give fire-flank the larger weight for studio-like halls.
- **Seats face the fire:** in 8 of 9 rooms with seats near a fire, some seats are turned toward it (benches facing
  across the pit, stools in an arc, thrones and armchairs angled, sofas in a U). Confirms the focal rule.
- **High seat:** Dragonsreach puts the throne on a dais at the far end across the axis, facing the door; Jorrvaskr has a
  table across the far end; one Valheim build has two thrones by the fire on the near side. Far end 2 of 3, mid long
  wall 0 of 3. Supports `far-mid` (dais) and gives no support in games for `long-mid`, which rests only on Hurstwic.
- **Tables run along the long axis:** 7 of 8 long tables (Jorrvaskr, Dragonsreach, Skellige longhouse 2, Kingfisher
  rows, Hearthfire main hall, Alvor house). Exception: a W3 dining table parallel to a wall fireplace. Confirms
  `table.alongLong`.
- **Home with one main room (far-sleep):** Alvor house shows the fire on one long wall, the table on the other, the
  beds at the far end and an open lane of rugs down the middle. Hearthfire small house and the Skellige hut put the
  fire in the middle with bed, table and storage on the walls. Confirms `far-sleep` and `hearth-ring`.
- **Table near the fire:** in homes the dining table stands within 1 to 3 m of the fire in 4 of 5 (Breezehome,
  Hearthfire main hall, Warmaiden's corner, Candlehearth). Confirms `table: fire` in the hearth-ring and box-bed
  schemes, and suggests a smaller weight for the far-sleep scheme's `table: mid` when the room has a central fire.

### Kitchens and cooking areas

6 pictures: W3 cookhouse, Bannered Mare kitchen, Dragonsreach kitchen, Valheim kitchen, Breezehome hall fire,
Hearthfire small house.

- In 3 of 6 the cooking fire is also the room's hearth (cookhouse, Breezehome, Hearthfire small house). Confirms "in
  a small house the hearth is the kitchen".
- Work table against the wall beside the fire: 2 (Bannered Mare, cookhouse counter). Work table or counter as an
  island in front of the fire: 2 (Dragonsreach, Valheim counter). Suggest a kitchen scheme "island" next to
  `triangle`.
- Food or pots hung over or beside the fire (meat, herbs, garlic, pans on a rail, meat racks): 4 of 6. No current rule;
  it is a clear "who works here" sign.
- Stores: barrels under the stair (Bannered Mare), heaps of tubs and baskets in corners (cookhouse), shelves of pottery
  on the wall (cookhouse, Bannered Mare, Breezehome). Confirms storage at corners and walls; adds "under the stair".
- All stations close together: Valheim kitchen within 2 to 3 m, cookhouse stools and stump right at the hearth.
  Confirms the 5 m cauldron range and the triangle idea.

### Tavern common rooms

9 pictures: Elfsong 4 floors, Kingfisher, Bannered Mare, Sleeping Giant, Candlehearth, Valheim bar.

- **Bar at the back or side, facing the room and the door end: 4 of 4** where a bar was visible (Elfsong, Bannered
  Mare, Kingfisher, Valheim). Confirms `bar-back` and `bar-side`.
- **Lane behind the counter with shelves, barrels or a cupboard on the wall: 3 of 4.** The counter stands about 1 to
  1.5 m off the wall (Elfsong, Bannered Mare) or against it with shelves above (Valheim). Suggest a keeper lane of
  about 1 m behind the counter and storage on the wall behind, in place of storage only in the `far` zone.
- **Stools in a row in front of the counter: 3 of 4**, often on a rug that marks the bar zone (Elfsong).

## Patterns across all rooms

- **Big pieces square to the walls, small pieces loose.** In every studio room, beds, tables, counters and stations are
  square to the walls. Stools, chairs and cushions near a fire or a table stand a little off line in about half the
  rooms (Elfsong stools, Skellige stools, Valheim thrones, WoW armchairs). Confirms the 0.15 m and 10 degree limit for
  anchors and the looser limit for light pieces.
- **Open floor share:** studio rooms 45 to 70%, middle of the range about 55% (12 rooms). One crowded inn 35%. ESO
  player homes about 30%. Confirms `walkableShare: [0.45, 0.65]`. The ESO pictures show what a room looks like when a
  decorator fills every wall and surface: it reads as a hoard.
- **Runner rugs mark the lanes:** 7 places (Elfsong 2, Bannered Mare, Jorrvaskr, Valheim longhouse, Hearthfire hall,
  WoW). **Round rugs mark a seating group or a clear center:** 8 places. This fits the rule that rugs may lie on lanes,
  and suggests the planner lay runners along the main lane in halls and taverns and a round rug under each seating
  group.
- **Zones are split by level and posts more than by walls:** steps or a raised or sunken floor 6 (Elfsong 2, Bannered
  Mare, Jorrvaskr, Dragonsreach, BG3 study), post rows 5, railings 3, curtains or screens 3, a lattice partition 1.
  Valheim has floor pieces at several heights and posts, so these dividers are in reach.
- **Clutter sits:** on tables in 2 to 5 items per table (most rooms); at work spots (bowls at the hearth curb, a bucket
  and cloths by the sick bed, a bottle by the bar, a broom on the bench); in heaps in corners (cookhouse, Skellige hut,
  ESO); overhead (herbs and meat over the fire, drying fish, ropes and baskets in the rafters) in 5 rooms; on the wall
  above anchors (shields over beds, trophy heads high on long walls and posts, banners flanking fireplaces and over
  beds). The open middle of the floor holds nothing but rugs in all studio rooms. The JSON has no overhead or "wall
  above the anchor" layer; both are cheap ways to add the lived-in look without using floor.
- **Story spots seen:** the sick man with bucket and cloths (Last Light Inn), toys on the workshop bench (Arfur), the
  trophy head over the fireplace (Elfsong), the alchemy corner (Breezehome), trophy heads in a hunter-smith's house
  (Alvor), the stage (Elfsong, Kingfisher), the children's room with two small beds (Hearthfire). They sit at the focal
  point or at a work spot in view of the main lane in 6 of 7. Confirms the "story spot" in view of the main lane.
- **Variance:** within each game, two rooms of the same type share kit pieces and each has its own plan (Skyrim
  inns 3 plans in 3 inns; Elfsong 3 floors, 3 plans; Skyrim bedrooms 3 kinds; BG3 forges closed and open). Confirms
  the variance drivers and the advice that players notice repeated detail first.

## Summary of changes the pictures suggest for room-layouts.json

1. Draw `orient.bed.sideToWall` from bed size and room width: single beds in small rooms or under eaves 0.6 to 0.8;
   double beds 0.1 to 0.3 with both long sides open and a nightstand each side.
2. Add a guest and children's scheme with beds side by side on one wall, 1.0 to 2.0 m apart with a nightstand or desk
   between.
3. Soften `bed.nearFire` in the `home` role: penalty only inside about 1.5 m of the fire; beds on a wall facing the
   fire from 2 to 5 m are normal in one-room homes and longhouses.
4. Add a `fire-flank` zone for tables and benches in halls and taverns, parallel to a long fire, 0.5 to 1.5 m from it;
   keep `long-walls` for sleeping platforms and storage in longhouses.
5. Give the dais or far-end high seat more weight than the mid-long-wall seat unless the culture asks for it.
6. In taverns, leave about 1 m behind the counter for the keeper, put storage on the wall behind it, and keep a small
   performer spot.
7. Add a `wall-seats` tavern or hall scheme with seats against walls and posts facing a central fire.
8. Add an `island` kitchen scheme and allow food and pots hung over or beside the fire.
9. Set the forge to anvil gap at 1.0 to 1.8 m; let the smithy's near side be fully open.
10. Add "under the stair" and "loft or rafters" as storage spots, and a "wall above the anchor" spot (over bed heads,
    fireplaces, counters) for shields, banners, trophies and shelves.
11. Lay runner rugs along main lanes in halls and taverns and a round rug under each seating group.
